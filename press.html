What makes it different

  • The search runs on the phone. Three passes — a perceptual hash, Vision's FeaturePrint and a library-wide search over those vectors — with PhotoKit, Vision and Core Image. Nothing is uploaded, there is no account and no server. The app works in airplane mode.
  • It is honest about the space it frees. A video can keep a 1080p copy instead of disappearing. On a library synced with iCloud Photos, the app shows how much of the freed space actually sat on the iPhone rather than adding two numbers that overlap.
  • No scare screens. No invented junk counters, no red storage bars, no permission asked before it is explained. The app opens on the user's own photographs.

Short, 50 words

Keepick is a photo cleaner for iPhone. It finds near-duplicate frames of one moment, screenshots, blurry shots and heavy videos, groups them into small packs and lets you decide one card at a time with a swipe. The whole search runs on the device: no account, no upload, no server.

Long, 100 words

Keepick is a photo cleaner for iPhone, built by one independent developer. Three passes run over the library on the device itself — a perceptual hash for exact repeats, Vision's FeaturePrint for frames of the same scene, and a library-wide search that catches crops and re-saves taken years apart. What comes out is a pack of eight or ten frames of one moment rather than a list of thousands: swipe up to keep, down to trash, with the system confirming every deletion. Videos can keep a 1080p copy instead of vanishing, and "On this day" turns the same library into a reason to come back.
